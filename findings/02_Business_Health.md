# BUSINESS HEALTH

## REVENUE OVERVIEW

This analysis uses delivered orders only as the financial population. Order status is normalized with LOWER(TRIM(status)) because the dataset contains inconsistent capitalization such as delivered, DELIVERED, and Delivered.

The business has 19,979 delivered orders generating $150.42M in recorded order revenue. Monthly revenue is volatile rather than consistently increasing: revenue was $35.70M in March, increased sharply to $66.62M in April, and then declined to $38.05M in May.
Although June also shows a substantial decline, it should not be interpreted as deteriorating business performance because the available data only covers part of the month. June shows a drop, but this doesn’t mean business is getting worse, the data only covers part of the month.

## REVENUE TRAJECTORY 

Monthly revenue analysis shows that the business is not experiencing steady linear growth.

The largest movement occurred between March and April. Delivered orders increased from 4,762 to 8,718, while revenue increased from $35.70M to $66.62M. This represents approximately 83% order growth and 87% revenue growth. AOV changed only slightly, from $7,497.79 to $7,641.43, indicating that the April revenue increase was primarily associated with higher order volume rather than substantially higher spending per order.

The reverse pattern appears in May. Orders fell to 5,104 and revenue fell to $38.05M, while AOV remained broadly stable at $7,454.47. This suggests that order volume is the main factor behind the observed month-to-month revenue movement.

## AVERAGE ORDER VALUE

The overall Average Order Value (AOV) for delivered orders is $7,528.82. On average, each delivered order contains 2.65 items, and this number stays fairly steady month to month (between 2.61 and 2.67). This consistency shows that changes in cart size are not the main reason for revenue fluctuations.

Looking at order values, the distribution is right‑skewed. The median order value is about $4,597, while the average is $7,529. This gap means that a smaller number of very high‑value orders are pulling the average upward.

## KEY OBSERVATIONS

Revenue changes are primarily volume-driven. April was the strongest complete month because substantially more orders were delivered, while AOV and items per order remained broadly stable. May's decline similarly coincided with lower order volume. Because both months represent complete reporting periods, this change reflects a genuine shift in business performance. 

The next business question should focus on what caused this decline, whether it was reduced marketing activity, weaker customer acquisition, lower repeat purchases, or seasonal demand patterns
